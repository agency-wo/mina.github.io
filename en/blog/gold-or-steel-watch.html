Gold or Steel? What Plated Gold Really Means at This Price

No watch under a few hundred euros is solid gold, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something. Here is what gold-tone actually is, how long it lasts, what we can and cannot do when it wears, and when steel is simply the better buy.

8 min read Iglisi Watch - Durrës August 2026

Roughly four in ten of the watches on our counter are gold-tone or two-tone, and the same question comes with almost every one of them: will the gold wear off. It deserves a proper answer rather than a reassuring noise, because the answer changes which watch you should buy. So here it is, in the order that matters.

First, the Part Nobody Should Dodge

No watch at this price is solid gold. Not ours, not anyone's. What you are buying is a thin layer of gold-coloured finish bonded to a steel or brass case, and that is entirely normal for anything short of several hundred euros. Solid gold at 6,800 Lek is not a bargain, it is a lie, and if you see it advertised that is the most useful warning sign you will get all day.

What plating buys you is the look, honestly and cheaply. The Daniel Klein Premium at 6,200 Lek is gold-plated with a silver sunburst dial and a coin-edge bezel, and across a room it does exactly what a far more expensive dress watch does. That is a real thing to want and there is nothing wrong with wanting it. The only requirement is that you know what you have.

How Long It Actually Lasts

On a watch worn most days, expect five years or more before the plating starts to show wear, and on a watch kept for the office, dinners and occasions it may well never show it at all. That is not a sales answer. It is what walks back through our door.

When it does go, it goes in predictable places: the clasp first, then the edges of the bracelet links, because those are where metal rubs metal every time the watch goes on and off. The case usually looks fine long after the bracelet has started to fade, which tells you something useful about how these watches age.

The realistic picture. Five years of daily wear is a long time for a 6,300 Lek watch. If the plating on the Navimarine NM001101 at 6,300 Lek finally starts to show at the clasp in year six, that watch has done its job several times over. The mistake is not buying plated gold. The mistake is buying plated gold and then treating it like a dive watch.

And When It Goes, It Is Permanent

This is the part you will not read on most shop websites, so we will say it plainly: we do not re-plate watches, and worn plating cannot be repaired. There is no polish, no service and no product that puts it back. Polishing a plated case actively makes it worse, because polishing removes material and the material is the plating.

That is exactly why the buying decision matters more here than anywhere else in the shop. A scratched steel case can be brought back. A worn plated case cannot. So the question is not really whether plating lasts, it is whether the way you will wear this watch is kind to it.

The Rules That Make It Last

None of these are difficult, and together they are the difference between five years and considerably more.

  • Keep it out of the sea. Salt and sunscreen are specifically what eat plating, and we see the results every August. A gold-tone watch is a bad beach watch even when its water rating is fine, because the rating protects the movement, not the finish.
  • Leave it off at the gym. Sweat plus the constant knocks of weights is the fastest wear there is.
  • Perfume, cleaning sprays and solvents go on before the watch, not near it. They attack the surface finish and can discolour plating permanently.
  • Clean it with warm water and a soft cloth, and dry it. Never a cleaning product, never a polishing compound.

Our cleaning guide covers the method for every material, and the summer and sea guide explains why August is when we see the damage.

When Steel Is Simply the Better Buy

If the watch is going to be worn every day, near water, or at work with your hands, buy steel and stop thinking about it. Steel has nothing to lose: no layer to wear through, no finish to protect, and a scratch in it can be brushed back rather than being permanent. Thirty-three of the fifty-eight watches we stock are steel, so this is not a compromise choice.

The Navimarine NM182-01 at 6,800 Lek gives you a fluted bezel and a jubilee bracelet in plain steel, which is the dressy look without the fragile finish. If water is genuinely part of your life, the Navimarine Steel 5ATM Blue at 7,300 Lek has 5 ATM printed on the dial. And the Casio A159WA at 5,900 Lek is the extreme case of the argument: a steel watch we see come in twenty years old, scratched to a matte grey and still running.

Two-Tone: the Middle Path, With One Caveat

Two-tone mixes gold-coloured and steel elements, and it is the most popular compromise on our counter for good reason: it reads as dressier than plain steel while putting far less plated surface in harm's way. The Navimarine NM282-04 at 6,300 Lek has a cushion-square case with a deep green dial, and the Navimarine NM268-06 at 8,200 Lek is the most jewellery-like piece we stock.

The caveat is simple and worth saying: the gold-coloured parts of a two-tone watch are still plated and still wear. What you gain is that the steel parts do not, so the watch ages unevenly rather than uniformly. Most people find that acceptable. It is worth knowing before rather than after.

Gold at the Top of the Range

If you want the gold look on something built to a different standard, the Hislon Classic is where our range ends. The Hislon Classic at 18,300 Lek is full gold-tone with a fluted bezel and a sapphire crystal, and the Hislon Classic CL214S-04SS at 19,300 Lek is the two-tone version. They are still plated, because that is what gold-coloured steel is, but they pair it with a Swiss movement and a crystal that resists the scratches mineral glass picks up, so the watch as a whole stays looking new for longer.

That is the honest ladder. More money does not buy solid gold at any point in our range. It buys a better watch underneath the same finish, which we set out in full in what more money actually buys.

The One-Line Answer

Buy gold-tone if it is for the office, dinners and occasions, and you will likely never see the plating go. Buy steel if it is going on your wrist every morning and coming off at midnight, because steel forgives everything plating does not. Frequently Asked Questions

Is a gold-tone watch at this price real gold?

No. No watch at this price is solid gold, ours included. Gold-tone means a thin layer of gold-coloured finish bonded to a steel or brass case, which is normal for anything short of several hundred euros. That plating gives you the look honestly and affordably. If anyone offers you solid gold at 6,800 Lek, that is the clearest warning sign you will get.

How long before the gold wears off?

On a watch worn most days, five years or more before it starts to show, and on a watch kept for the office and occasions it may never show at all. When it does go it goes at the clasp first, then the edges of the bracelet links, because that is where metal rubs metal every time the watch goes on and off. The case usually still looks fine long after the bracelet has started to fade.

Can worn plating be repaired or re-plated?

No. We do not re-plate watches and worn plating cannot be brought back. There is no polish, service or product that restores it, and polishing a plated case makes it worse rather than better because polishing removes material and the material is the plating. That is exactly why choosing between gold-tone and steel matters at the point of purchase rather than afterwards.

Can I wear a gold-tone watch in the sea?

We would not. Salt and sunscreen are specifically what attack plating, and we see the damage every August at a bench ten minutes from the beach. The water resistance rating on the dial protects the movement, not the finish, so a watch can be perfectly rated for a swim and still come out looking worse for it. Take a steel watch to the beach and leave the gold-tone one at home.

How do I make the plating last?

Four things. Keep it out of the sea and off at the gym, because salt and sweat plus knocks are what wear it. Put perfume, cleaning sprays and solvents on before the watch rather than near it, since they can discolour the finish permanently. Clean it with warm water and a soft cloth and then dry it. Never use a cleaning product or a polishing compound on a plated case.

Should I buy gold-tone or steel?

Gold-tone if the watch is for the office, dinners and occasions, where you will most likely never see the plating go. Steel if it goes on every morning and comes off at midnight, or if water and hands-on work are part of your life, because steel has no layer to lose and a scratch in it can be brushed back.
