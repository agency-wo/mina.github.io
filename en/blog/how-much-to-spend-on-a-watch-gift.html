How Much to Spend on a Watch as a Birthday Gift

Everybody answers this with etiquette and nobody answers it with money. Here is what a watch gift actually costs for each person you might be buying for, from a counter in Durrës.

6 min read August 2026

Search this question and you get etiquette. Percentages of your salary, rules about how long you have known someone, one jeweller opening the watch bracket at two thousand euro. None of it is a number you can act on in Albania. So here is the version with prices in it, from a counter that runs from €50 to €249.

The Honest Answer, First

Almost every birthday gift that leaves this shop costs between 4,600 and 7,100 Lek. That is the band, and a watch from inside it does not look like a compromise: steel, a date, a bracelet we size for free, brand new with a 1-year guarantee. Everything below is about when to go above that band and when to stay under it, and the answer turns out to depend on the relationship rather than on the watch.

The rule that actually works. Spend more when the relationship is closer or the birthday is a round number. Spend less when you do not know the person well, because an expensive watch from somebody you barely know reads as awkward rather than generous. That is the whole etiquette, and it costs nothing to follow.

For a Parent

The one relationship where people reliably spend more, and the one where it is worth it. A parent is likely to wear the same watch for a decade, which makes sapphire crystal the thing you are actually buying: it is the part that gets scratched, so it is the part that decides whether the watch still looks new later.

The Hislon Classic Queen at 169 euro (15,600 L) is full gold-tone steel with a mother-of-pearl dial, crystal-set indices, sapphire crystal and blued hands. The Hislon Classic in steel at 199 euro (18,400 L) is the understated one: fluted bezel, horizontal-stripe silver dial, sapphire, date at three.

For a Partner

Closer than a friend, but this is the case where getting the style wrong costs you more than getting the price wrong. Buy what they already wear rather than what you would like to see them in, and if you are unsure, send us a photo of the watch on their wrist in any old picture and we will tell you which of ours is nearest.

The Philippe Lauren Steel Chronograph at 72 euro (6,600 L) is polished steel with a black dial and three counters. The Daniel Klein Premium Gold Tonneau at 75 euro (6,900 L) is a gold-tone dress watch with a tonneau case, a fine wave-textured silver dial and blued hands. The POLOTIME Gold Crystal at 76 euro (7,000 L) is gold-tone with a champagne sunray dial and crystal-set indices.

For a Sibling or a Close Friend

The volume band, and the one most gifts come from. Enough that it reads as considered, not so much that it puts them under an obligation.

The Philippe Lauren Steel Classic at 58 euro (5,400 L) is the safest thing on the counter for somebody whose taste you are not sure of. The Daniel Klein Premium at 64 euro (5,900 L) is gold-plated with a silver sunburst dial, applied gold bar indices and a coin-edge bezel. The Bigotti Gold Square Crystal at 77 euro (7,100 L) is a square gold-tone case with a crystal-set bezel on all four sides and a glossy black dial.

For a Colleague, or When You Barely Know Them

Here the goal is to not create a debt. A watch that costs too much from somebody at work is a problem for the person receiving it, and the fix is not a worse watch, it is a cheaper one that still looks right.

The Belonni Collection at 50 euro (4,600 L) is the cheapest all-steel watch we list: white dial, Arabic numerals, date at three, coin-edge bezel, five-link bracelet. The Casio MTP-VD01 at 52 euro (4,800 L) is the steel sports-dress alternative, black dial, luminous batons, 50m water resistance and a name everybody recognises.

Nobody has to know what you paid. We can leave the price off everything that goes with the watch, so the person receiving it never sees the figure. That matters most in exactly this band, and it costs nothing.

What the Extra Money Buys

Going up a tier buys sapphire crystal, applied rather than printed indices, a dial with real texture and a bracelet that tapers. It does not buy better timekeeping: a quartz watch at 50 euro (4,600 L) and one at 249 euro (23,000 L) keep time the same. The Hislon Women Classic Queen at 149 euro (13,700 L) is the clearest example of the jump, and the Cortébert Black Bezel Chronograph at 249 euro (23,000 L) is the top of our counter. We take the whole ladder apart in what more money actually buys in a watch.

What It Costs to Keep

Worth adding to the price before you compare anything. A quartz watch needs a battery every 18 to 24 months and we charge 200 to 600 Lek for it depending on the watch, about two minutes while you wait, no appointment. Our battery replacement page has the detail. That is the entire running cost, which is the argument for buying one good watch rather than replacing a cheap one every year.

Where the Numbers Come From
