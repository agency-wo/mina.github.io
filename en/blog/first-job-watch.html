A Watch for Your First Job: Buying One With Your Own Money

A graduation watch is handed to you across a table. This one you choose, and you pay for it out of a salary you earned. That changes the question, and it changes the answer.

5 min read August 2026

A graduation watch arrives wrapped. Somebody else set the budget, somebody else chose it, and your job was to look pleased. The watch you buy when the first salary lands is a different object. Nobody is watching, the budget is yours, and you are the one who has to live with it every morning for years.

In short: a steel quartz watch at 70 euro (6,500 L), plus a battery every couple of years, costs under 1,000 Lekë a year to own across ten years. That figure, not the price on the tag, is the honest way to judge a watch you are buying with your own money.

A Gift and a Purchase Are Different Decisions

We covered the gift side properly in our guide to graduation watch ideas, and it does a job this article does not. It works out what a family should spend so that the watch reads as an occasion, and it lands on the Hislon bracket for good reasons: an occasion gift has to look like one when it comes out of the box in front of relatives.

Buying it yourself reverses every part of that. Nobody needs your watch to look ceremonial. It needs to survive a commute, a desk or a workshop, and still be on your wrist in five years without you having thought about it much. That is a cheaper question than the gift one, and it is answered with different watches.

What Ten Years of Owning It Actually Costs

Almost nothing written about first watches mentions the running cost, which is odd, because a quartz watch has exactly one. The battery. We change them at 200 to 600 Lekë depending on the cell, roughly every couple of years, and that is the only scheduled expense the watch will ever put in front of you.

So run it on a steel watch at 70 euro (6,500 L), worn for ten years. Five battery changes puts the whole cost of ownership somewhere between 7,800 and 9,800 Lekë. That is under 1,000 Lekë a year to own a steel watch outright, and even on a pessimistic five-year view it lands near 1,500 Lekë a year. The full arithmetic, along with the order things actually fail in, is in how long a 70 euro watch lasts. The short version of that order: the battery first, then the strap or bracelet, then the crown. The movement is not in the top three.

Why we are willing to publish that number. The bench that changes the battery is in the same room as the counter that sold you the watch. A shop that only sells is quoting a running cost it never has to honour.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I spend on a watch out of my first salary?

Less than most people expect, and the reason is arithmetic rather than modesty. A steel quartz watch at 70 euro (6,500 L) costs under 1,000 Lekë a year to own across ten years with the battery counted, because the battery is the only routine cost a quartz watch has. Spending three times that does not make a watch last three times longer. It buys a better crystal, a better finish and a better movement, which are worth paying for later rather than in the month you also pay a deposit.

Is a 70 euro quartz watch going to last, or will I just be replacing it?

Five to ten years in normal use, and that comes off our own bench rather than out of a brochure. A quartz movement has no mainspring under tension and no oil film to break down, so it keeps running as long as it gets a battery. Where you land in that range depends on water, knocks and whether the battery is changed before it dies, not on what you paid for the watch.

Should I wait and buy a Hislon instead?

If the money is genuinely spare, yes. The Hislon Masterwork at 184 euro (17,000 L) has a sapphire crystal and a level of finish you can see across a table, and it will still look right in ten years.
