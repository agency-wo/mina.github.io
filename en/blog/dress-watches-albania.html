Dress Watches in Albania: How to Buy One That Looks Expensive

The dress watch is the largest style on our counter and the easiest to get wrong. Slim case, clean dial, applied markers, and the way the bracelet meets the case. Here is what to check, and what each detail costs in Durrës.

8 min read September 2026

The dress watch is the largest style on this counter and the hardest thing in a shop window to judge. Two of them at the same price can look a class apart, and the difference is almost never the name on the dial. It is the case profile, the dial finish, whether the markers are applied or printed, and how the bracelet meets the case. This is what we look at before we put one out, priced in what we actually charge.

The short version: a slim case, one finish on the dial, applied markers, and a bracelet that sits flush against the case. Philippe Lauren Steel Classic at 58 euro (5,400 L) does all four at the bottom of the range, Bigotti Steel Sunburst at 82 euro (7,600 L) does them in a men's case, and Hislon Classic CL213S-02SS at 199 euro (18,400 L) does them under a sapphire crystal.

What a Dress Watch Is Being Asked to Do

A dress watch has one job: look right with a shirt and stay out of the way the rest of the time. Nothing to rotate, nothing to time, no bezel doing arithmetic. That is exactly why it gets judged on finish instead of function, and why a cheap one gives itself away in about a second. Our companion piece on the details that read as expensive covers where those details came from. This page is the buying half.

Slim Is the First Thing to Check

Thickness is the tell that survives every photograph. A dress case sits flat and slides under a cuff; a deep one rolls, catches the fabric and reads as a sports watch wearing the wrong clothes. Look for the side-profile shot before the dial shot, because that is the one nobody flatters.

Navimarine NMM1011 at 60 euro (5,500 L) is the slim minimalist end of it: steel, a pale ice-blue dial, applied baton indices, a polished steel bracelet, quartz movement and no date window to clutter the dial. Navimarine NM001101 in steel at 65 euro (6,000 L) is the same slim case with a black dial, fine baton indices and a date.

Daniel Klein F at 60 euro (5,500 L) puts a soft white sunburst dial and rose gold-tone stick indices in a slim steel case, and Daniel Klein Mesh at the same 60 euro (5,500 L) puts a slim gold-plated case on a woven Milanese bracelet. At the far end of the counter, Hislon Classic Queen QL145T-09SG at 189 euro (17,400 L) holds the slim profile in rose gold-tone, with a sapphire crystal and a quartz movement.

If you would rather be handed one than work down the list, this is the dress watch we would put in front of you first.

Sunburst or Flat: What the Dial Finish Decides

A sunburst dial is brushed outward from the centre in fine lines, so it takes light in a fan and changes as you turn your arm. It is the cheapest way a dial has ever found to look expensive, and it is also why a sunburst photographs worse than it looks. A flat dial holds one tone all day and hands the whole job to the markers. A textured dial, a fine wave or a stripe, sits between the two and catches light without moving.

For sunburst, Philippe Lauren Steel Classic at 58 euro (5,400 L) is silver sunburst with applied slim baton indices, a thin sweep seconds hand, a five-link polished bracelet and no date. Navimarine NM232-04 at 65 euro (6,000 L) does it in black, with applied batons, a five-link jubilee-style bracelet and a quartz movement. Bigotti Steel Sunburst at 82 euro (7,600 L) is the men's version: silver sunburst inside a polished chapter ring, applied faceted batons, a date at three, a fluted crown and a three-link bracelet.

For a dial that stays still, Daniel Klein Premium Roman at 69 euro (6,400 L) is a cream face with black Roman numerals and blued hands. Bigotti Two Tone Roman at 74 euro (6,800 L) and Daniel Klein Premium Gold Tonneau at 75 euro (6,900 L) both use a fine wave texture instead, which is the quietest of the three finishes and the one that dates slowest.

Applied Markers, and How to Read a Product Photo

An applied marker is a separate piece of metal fixed to the dial. It stands up, catches light on its facets and throws a small shadow. A printed marker is ink and does neither. Enlarge any product photo and look at the marker at nine o'clock: a shadow under it means applied, and applied is most of the reason one dial looks dearer than another.

Daniel Klein Premium Steel Crystal at 59 euro (5,400 L) sets applied batons at twelve, three, six and nine with eight crystal-set markers between them, on a three-link steel bracelet, no date, quartz movement. Philippe Lauren Steel Rectangular at 58 euro (5,400 L) is a rectangular case with bevelled cut corners, a silver sunburst dial, applied slim batons and a steel bracelet with visible screws on the links. Navimarine NM001101 in gold-tone at 65 euro (6,000 L) keeps fine baton indices and a date on a champagne dial.

Printed is not automatically cheap. A Roman numeral is printed on purpose, because a raised one turns into clutter at that size, and the Premium Roman above is the proof of it.

Roman, Baton or Arabic

Baton markers are the default and the safest buy. They say nothing, they suit a shirt and a t-shirt equally, and they are the reason a plain dial still looks current after five years.

Roman numerals are the formal option, and they take up more dial, so the watch reads dressier and slightly older. Navimarine NM229-06 at 75 euro (6,900 L) is a square two-tone with a white dial, black Roman numerals and screw details on the bezel. Philippe Lauren Gold Square Crystal at 59 euro (5,400 L) puts the same numerals in a gold-tone square with a crystal-set bezel. Navimarine NM282-04 at 65 euro (6,000 L) is a cushion-square two-tone with a deep green dial, and Navimarine Steel 5ATM Blue at 75 euro (6,900 L) is the one that works for an office and a beach at once: deep blue sunburst with Roman hour markers, a date at three and a stated 5 ATM.

Arabic numerals are the least common of the three on this part of the counter. Belonni Collection at 50 euro (4,600 L) is where to find them: all stainless steel, a clean white dial with Arabic markers, a date at three, a fluted coin-edge bezel, a five-link Jubilee-style bracelet and a 3 ATM rating. It is the cheapest watch on this page.

Date or No Date

A dial with no date is symmetrical, and symmetry is most of what makes a dress watch look considered. A date is simply useful. There is no right answer, only a preference, so here is each side of it.

Dateless: Philippe Lauren Gold Classic at 59 euro (5,400 L), a small round gold-tone case with a silver sunburst dial, slim gold-tone batons and a five-link bracelet. Bigotti Steel Square Crystal at 77 euro (7,100 L), a square case with a crystal-set bezel on all four sides, a silver sunburst dial, applied batons, a five-link bracelet and a quartz movement. Philippe Lauren Steel Crystal Blue at 52 euro (4,800 L), a crystal-set bezel in two rows over a deep blue sunburst.

With a date: Daniel Klein Premium at 64 euro (5,900 L), gold-plated, silver sunburst, applied gold bar indices, a fluted coin-edge bezel, a Roman numeral chapter ring and the date at three. Navimarine NVM112-B4 at 70 euro (6,500 L), full gold-tone with a faceted bezel, a champagne sunburst dial, a date and a quartz movement. Starking Classic Gold at 74 euro (6,800 L), a gold-tone tonneau case with a textured champagne dial, crystal-set hour accents, a date at six and a slim quartz movement.

One thing to check on any dated dial: whether the window is cut cleanly or eats into a marker, and whether the date disc matches the dial colour. A white disc on a black dial is a bright little square you will notice from across a room.

How the Bracelet Finishes

The bracelet is half the watch and the half people forget to look at. Start where it meets the case. An end link that follows the case line and closes the gap reads expensive; a straight bar with daylight on either side is the cheapest detail on any watch, at any price. Then look at the pattern, because that is what you will see every day.

A five-link jubilee is the dressiest and the one that catches the most light: Daniel Klein Premium Roman at 69 euro (6,400 L) on a solid five-link, Navimarine NM232-04 at 65 euro (6,000 L) on a jubilee-style, Bigotti Gold Black Dial at 69 euro (6,400 L) on a gold-tone five-link under a glossy black dial. A three-link is plainer and reads heavier: Daniel Klein Premium Black Dial at 65 euro (6,000 L).

Daniel Klein Mesh at 60 euro (5,500 L) is the woven Milanese answer, which sits closer to jewellery than to a watch strap. An integrated bracelet, where the links grow out of the case with no lugs between them, normally starts much further up a price list: Philippe Lauren Gold Sunray Crystal at 58 euro (5,400 L) with a notched bezel, Casio MTP-B145D at 89 euro (8,200 L) with a green sunburst dial, a faceted bezel, luminous batons, a date at three, 50m water resistance and a Japanese quartz movement, and Navimarine NAVI S046-COL 4 at 92 euro (8,500 L) with an octagonal bezel, a deep blue ribbed dial, hour and minute hands only, no date and a stated 5 ATM.

Leather is the traditional dress answer and we keep it at the top of the range: Cortébert Oval Moonphase at 141 euro (13,000 L) on black leather with a pin buckle. If you plan to change a strap yourself, the lug width guide explains the measurement to ask for.

Steel, Gold-Tone or Two-Tone

Every gold watch here is plated stainless steel. None of it is solid gold, and you should hear that from us rather than work it out later. What plating actually is and the whole gold-tone range are covered separately; what matters for a dress watch is that the colour changes who the watch is for.

Steel is the one that goes everywhere. Navimarine NM001101 in steel at 65 euro (6,000 L) is the minimal version, Bigotti Steel Sunburst at 82 euro (7,600 L) the fuller one, and Hislon Classic CL213S-02SS at 199 euro (18,400 L) the considered one, with a fluted bezel, a horizontal-stripe silver dial, a sapphire crystal and a date window.

Gold-tone reads as jewellery from across a room. Citizen Quartz GN at 60 euro (5,500 L) is a gold-plated case and bracelet with a clean dial and a Japanese quartz movement. Bigotti Gold Black Dial at 69 euro (6,400 L) is the black-and-gold pairing that never dates, Navimarine NVM112-B4 at 70 euro (6,500 L) is the gold watch most people picture when they ask for one, and POLOTIME Gold Crystal at 76 euro (7,000 L) is the unusual one, a champagne sunray dial inside a white outer ring with crystal-set indices at four points and crystal-set panels where the bracelet meets the case.

Two-tone is the safer buy for somebody who does not usually wear gold, because the steel carries the design and the gold is an accent. Bigotti Two Tone Roman at 74 euro (6,800 L) and Navimarine NM229-06 at 75 euro (6,900 L) are the quiet versions, Navimarine NM268-06 at 85 euro (7,800 L) is the loud one with a curved case, a crystal-set bezel and a blue sunburst dial with gold-tone Roman numerals, and Hislon Queen Two Tone Black at 183 euro (16,900 L) and Hislon Classic CL214S-04SS at 199 euro (18,400 L) are the two that pair the mix with a sapphire crystal.

Crystal-Set Means Cut Glass, Not Diamonds

We say crystal-set and we never say diamonds, because they are not diamonds. They are cut glass stones fixed into a bezel, a dial or a bracelet, and at these prices that is the only honest word for them. What they do is real enough: they pick up every light in the room, which is why a crystal-set watch is the one that gets noticed at a table.

Navimarine NVM141-A1 at 65 euro (6,000 L) is the value pick, a crystal-set bezel over a champagne dial with gold-tone Roman numerals in a faceted steel case. Bigotti Gold Square Crystal at 77 euro (7,100 L) runs crystals down all four sides of a square bezel against a glossy black dial. Navimarine NMM1013 at 85 euro (7,800 L) puts crystal hour markers on a green malachite-look dial. At the top, Hislon Queen Crystal Bezel at 151 euro (13,900 L) has a crystal-set bezel, a beaded bracelet, a mother-of-pearl dial with green stone markers at twelve, three, six and nine, a green stone in the crown, a sapphire crystal, hour and minute hands only and no date.

Frequently Asked Questions

What actually makes a cheap dress watch look cheap?

Rarely the brand. Look at the case profile, the dial finish, the markers and the bracelet. A deep case will not sit under a cuff, a dial carrying two or three finishes at once looks busy, printed markers stay flat where applied ones catch the light, and an end link that leaves a gap where the bracelet meets the case is the cheapest detail on any watch. The markers are the quickest to check in a photograph, because a raised marker throws a small shadow and a printed one does not.

Sunburst or a flat dial for a dress watch?

A sunburst dial is brushed outward from the centre so it takes light in a fan, which means it changes as you turn your arm and reads dearer than it is. The Philippe Lauren Steel Classic at 58 euro (5,400 L) and the Bigotti Steel Sunburst at 82 euro (7,600 L) both do it. A flat dial, like the cream face of the Daniel Klein Premium Roman at 69 euro (6,400 L), holds one tone all day and lets the numerals carry the watch. Neither is better. A sunburst photographs worse than it looks and a flat dial photographs exactly as it looks.

Which of your dress watches have a sapphire crystal?

The Hislons, and the Romanson BH3054GBR at 74 euro (6,800 L), which is the cheapest of them. Every Hislon prints SAPPHIRE on the dial, so we publish that claim for the whole brand. For every other dress watch on this page we publish no crystal type at all, because we have not verified one, and we would rather say nothing than guess.

Date window or no date window?

A dial with no date is symmetrical and cleaner, which is why so many classic dress watches leave it off: the Philippe Lauren Gold Classic at 59 euro (5,400 L) and the Bigotti Steel Square Crystal at 77 euro (7,100 L) are both dateless. A date is simply useful, and on the Daniel Klein Premium at 64 euro (5,900 L) it sits at three inside a Roman numeral chapter ring. What to check in a photograph is whether the window is cut cleanly or eats into a marker, and whether the date disc matches the dial colour.
