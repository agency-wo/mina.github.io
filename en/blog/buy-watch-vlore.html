What a Vlorë Summer Actually Does to a Watch

Salt air is not a metaphor on that coast. From June to September there is a fine salt aerosol on everything within a few hundred metres of the water, and it settles on a watch whether or not the watch ever goes in the sea. On its own it does very little. Left to dry on the metal it is a different story, because what remains is a crust that holds moisture against the surface every night.

A plated case and bracelet is where it shows first. Gold-tone plating is a few microns thick, and it is thinnest at the edges of the bracelet links and at the lug corners, exactly the places that flex and rub. That is where wear-through appears, and salt accelerates it. This is not our theory: the description of the Navimarine NAVI 009-COL 6 at 95 euro (8,800 L) says it in one sentence, that keeping the plating away from salt water is what makes the finish last. We would rather write that on the product page than sell you a promise we cannot keep.

A leather strap has the shortest life of anything you can wear in Vlorë in August. Sweat gets into the grain, salt dries in it, and the sun does the rest, so the leather stiffens and then splits, usually at the spring bar holes rather than in the middle. A strap is a consumable and this is how consumables go. Our guide on caring for a watch strap covers the routine that buys you an extra season.

The gasket is the part nobody thinks about. The rubber seals at the caseback, the crown and the crystal are what any rating depends on, and they harden with heat and age. A rating is a measurement taken on a new watch in a factory, not a permanent property of the object. That matters more on a coast than anywhere else, and it is the reason the two sections below are written the way they are.

Every watch this button can land on prints an actual water resistance figure in its own description. That is the whole qualification for being here.

Silent Does Not Mean Waterproof

Everything not named above is silent on water, and silent means silent. We will not tell you that a Hislon, a Philippe Lauren, a Bigotti or a Daniel Klein is fine in the sea, because nothing in front of us says that it is. A dress watch with no stated rating usually has some minimal sealing and is fine under a summer shower of rain, and that is as far as anyone honest can go without a pressure tester and the manufacturer's paperwork.

The ratings that do exist are also read wrongly more often than not. Our explainer on what ATM, BAR and metres really mean goes through it properly, but the short version for a Vlorë beach is this. 3 ATM is rain and hand washing. 5 ATM is a swim at the surface, not a jump off the rocks, because impact with water multiplies the pressure for an instant. 100m is the group we would actually take into the sea. On any of them, never touch the crown or a chronograph pusher while the watch is wet, because that is how water gets in on a watch that was perfectly sealed a second earlier.

Our 1-year guarantee runs on top of any manufacturer warranty, and it says the same thing out loud: it excludes water damage beyond the rating printed on the dial. A guarantee that promised more would be a guarantee we would have to argue our way out of later, which helps nobody.

The one habit worth more than any rating. If the watch has been near the sea, rinse it under fresh tap water and dry it before it goes on the shelf for the night. That single minute does more for a bracelet and a gasket in Vlorë than any specification on the dial, and it is free.

If You Take It Off Before You Go In

Most people do exactly this, and it is the sensible answer for anything handsome. Once the watch is not going in the water, the rating stops being the question and the dial becomes the question.

The Hislon Classic CL213S-02SS at 199 euro (18,400 L) is the dress watch we sell most of: fluted bezel, horizontal-stripe silver dial, sapphire crystal and a date window. Sapphire is the part that matters on a coast for a different reason than water, because sand is the hardest thing your watch will meet all summer and a sapphire crystal shrugs off what mineral glass records forever.

The Romanson BH3054GBR at 74 euro (6,800 L) is Swiss quartz with sapphire crystal glass, a rectangular case, a sunburst dial and a date. It is fully gold-plated, so read the plating paragraph above and treat it accordingly: it will look superb in Vlorë and it wants rinsing and drying, not a night on the balcony with salt on it. The Philippe Lauren Steel Crystal Blue at 52 euro (4,800 L) is the ladies' equivalent, stainless steel with a crystal-set bezel in two rows and a deep blue sunburst dial, and steel has no plating to lose.
