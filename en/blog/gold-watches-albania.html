The largest part of our counter, sorted by what the gold actually is. Plated, not solid, and that is the first thing on the page.

What Gold-Tone Means on This Counter

Every gold watch we sell is plated stainless steel. None of them is solid gold, and we would rather you heard that from us than worked it out later. Plating is a thin gold-coloured layer bonded to a steel case and bracelet, which is why a watch that looks like jewellery costs what a steel watch costs.

That is not a warning, it is the point. Solid gold at these sizes would cost more than a car and would be too soft to wear every day. Plating gives you the colour, steel underneath, and a price you can hand over as a gift. What it will not do is survive scrubbing, swimming or the sea, and the section near the bottom of this page says exactly what to keep it away from.

Three words appear on our product pages and they are not interchangeable. Full gold-tone is case and bracelet both. Two-tone is steel with gold on part of it. Rose gold-tone is the pinker shade, which reads softer against the skin.

Rose Gold, Which Is Not the Same Thing

Rose gold-tone is pinker and warmer, and on most skin it reads quieter than yellow does. We keep two versions and they sit at opposite ends of the price list.

The Daniel Klein F at 60 euro (5,500 L) uses rose gold-tone only for the stick indices and hands, on a steel case with a soft white sunburst dial, so the pink is a detail rather than the whole watch. The Hislon Classic Queen QL145T-09SG at 189 euro (17,400 L) commits to it: rose gold-tone case and bracelet, slim profile, sapphire crystal.

Keeping the Plating

Plating wears where the watch is touched: the clasp, the edges of the bracelet links and the underside of the case. It does not wear evenly and it does not wear quickly, but it does wear, and knowing that is most of the battle.

Four things shorten it and all four are avoidable. Salt water is the worst, so a gold-tone watch is not a beach watch. Perfume and hairspray settle on the case and dull it. Abrasive cloths and metal polish will take the layer off, so a soft dry cloth is the entire cleaning routine. And sleeping in it doubles the hours of contact for no benefit at all.

We service every watch we sell, and we can replace a bracelet, a crystal or a battery. What no workshop can honestly promise is re-plating a worn case back to new, so the sensible plan is to keep it out of the sea and bring it in when the battery goes. There is more in our guide to cleaning a watch properly and in what strap and bracelet care actually involves.

So Which Gold Should You Buy?

If it is a gift and you do not know their taste, buy two-tone: it is the version someone who never wears gold will still put on. If they already wear gold jewellery, buy full gold-tone and let it match what is already there. If you want the finish to stay looking new for as long as possible, buy the Romanson or a Hislon, because the sapphire crystal keeps the face clear long after a mineral one has picked up marks.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are these real gold watches?

No, and we say so before anything else on the page. Every gold watch we sell is gold-plated stainless steel, which is a gold-coloured layer bonded over a steel case and bracelet. Solid gold at these sizes would cost many times the price and would be too soft for daily wear. You are buying the colour and the look, on a steel watch, with a one-year guarantee.

Will the gold wear off?

Eventually, at the points the watch is touched most: the clasp, the link edges and the underside of the case. It is slow, and much slower again if you keep it out of salt water, away from perfume and hairspray, and clean it with nothing more than a soft dry cloth. Polishes made for solid metal will take the plating off, so do not use them.

What is the difference between full gold-tone and two-tone?

Full gold-tone means the case and the bracelet are both gold-coloured, so the watch reads as one piece. Two-tone means a steel watch with gold on part of it, usually the bezel, the centre links or the indices. Two-tone is the easier one to wear if you do not normally wear gold, and it is the safer choice as a gift.

Which gold watches have a sapphire crystal?

The Hislon Classic and Classic Queen range, and the Romanson BH3054GBR, which at 74 euro is the cheapest sapphire crystal we stock. Everything else in the gold range has mineral glass. Sapphire is far harder to scratch, which is what keeps a watch face looking new.
