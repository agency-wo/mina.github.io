A Watch as a Birthday Gift: Choosing One in Albania

A birthday arrives while you are thinking about something else. Here is what to spend, what each amount actually buys on our counter in Durrës, and the three things we do to a watch before you hand it over.

7 min read August 2026

Everybody has a birthday every year, which is what makes it different from every other occasion we have written about. A wedding is planned months out. New Year is on a date you can see coming. A birthday turns up while you are thinking about something else and leaves you about a week. A watch answers that better than most things at this price, and badly in two specific cases. Both are below.

When a Watch Is the Right Gift, and When It Is Not

Take the case against it first, because it is short and it is real. If the person already wears one watch every day and loves it, a second watch is a harder gift than it looks. It goes in a drawer beside the one they actually reach for. And if you know nothing about their taste at all, a watch is a personal object and you would be guessing; there are better things to guess with.

Everything else is in favour. A watch is worn rather than stored, which very little else in this price range can claim. It does not need a size the way clothing does, and if the bracelet sits loose we take links out at the counter in a few minutes, free, including for a watch that arrived by courier rather than being bought in the shop. It survives being kept: a good share of the batteries we change are in watches somebody was given years ago.

What to Spend

Our counter runs from €50 to €249. Most birthday gifts come from the lower half of that and there is nothing wrong with it: the distance between the bottom of our range and the middle is finish and detail, not whether the watch keeps time or how long it lasts. The three bands below are the ones our shelf actually falls into, so there is no point pretending otherwise. If you want the reasoning behind what separates them, we set it out in what a watch actually costs at each tier.

Under 7,000 Lek

The deepest part of our counter, and where most birthday gifts come from.

The Philippe Lauren Steel Classic at 58 euro (5,400 L) is a plain steel dress watch, silver sunburst dial, applied baton indices, nothing shouting. It is the safest thing on the counter to give somebody whose taste you are not certain of.

The Daniel Klein F at 60 euro (5,500 L) is the same idea warmer: a soft white sunburst dial with rose gold-tone indices and hands, on a polished bracelet.

The Romanson BH3054GBR at 74 euro (6,800 L) is the one that punches out of its band. Swiss quartz, sapphire crystal, a full gold-plated rectangular case with a sunburst dial. Sapphire is unusual at this money and we only say it where it is true.

7,000 to 14,000 Lek

Where you go when the watch has to feel like it cost something.

The Casio MTP-B145D at 89 euro (8,200 L) has a striking green sunburst dial, a faceted bezel and an integrated steel bracelet. It reads as a considerably more expensive watch than it is.

The Daniel Klein Exclusive Chronograph at 89 euro (8,200 L) is a working chronograph: three subdials, deep blue sunburst dial, rotating dive-style bezel, luminous batons.

The Cortébert Oval Moonphase at 141 euro (13,000 L) is the one people stop at. Polished oval steel case, champagne dial, Roman numerals, a day subdial, a pointer date and a moon phase window at six.

Over 14,000 Lek

The ceremonial end, for a birthday that gets remembered rather than marked.

The Hislon Masterwork at 184 euro (17,000 L) is a sport-chic chronograph, jet-black dial, three subdials, red accents, sapphire crystal and a steel bracelet.

The Hislon Classic at 199 euro (18,400 L) is the quiet one: fluted bezel, horizontal-stripe silver dial, sapphire crystal, date at three.

The Cortébert Black Bezel Chronograph at 249 euro (23,000 L) sits at the top of our counter, with a black bezel held by six exposed screws and a two-tone bracelet.

Which Style Actually Suits Them

Style is a shorter question than it looks. Watch what they already wear rather than asking. Somebody in a suit most days is a dress watch; somebody outdoors is steel and a higher water rating; somebody who wears gold jewellery will not thank you for brushed steel. Our guide to dress, sport and casual watches is the long-form version of that paragraph.

The one shortcut that works. Send us a photo of the watch they wear now, or of their wrist on any old picture, and we will tell you which of ours is closest. It is the fastest way through this and it costs nothing.

If They Already Wear a Watch

This is the case where we would rather sell you less. If they already have a watch they love and it has stopped, or the strap has gone, or it has been in a drawer since the battery died, having it working again lands harder than a new one does. We are a repair bench before we are a shop: a battery takes about two minutes while you wait, and a strap is not much longer. Our piece on repairing or replacing a watch sets out where the line falls.
