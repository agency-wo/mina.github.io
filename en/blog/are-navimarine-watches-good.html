Are Navimarine Watches Good? An Honest Answer From the Bench

We sell Navimarine and we repair watches for a living, so we see them new in the box and again when something goes wrong. Here is the honest version: what a Navimarine is, what €60 to €107 buys, and the things we will not claim because we have not verified them.

"Are Navimarine watches good?" usually gets typed with a thumb already hovering over a purchase, so it deserves a straight answer rather than a sales page. We sell Navimarine and we repair watches for a living, which means we see them new in the box and again when their owner brings one back. Here is the honest version, including the parts that do not flatter the brand.

What Navimarine Actually Is

Navimarine is a value brand, not a heritage one. It is not Swiss. The Swiss-marked watches on our shelves are Hislon and Cortébert, and Navimarine is not one of them, so if a nautical-sounding name had you picturing an alpine workshop, correct that first. We are a dealer rather than the manufacturer, and we are not going to invent a founding date or a factory story for a brand whose history we cannot document. What we can describe is what comes out of the boxes, because we are the ones who open them.

What comes out is consistent. Solid stainless steel cases and bracelets. Quartz movements. Dial designs that borrow their shapes from watches costing several times more: a rotating dive-style bezel, a 24-hour bezel with a fourth arrow-tipped hand reading a second time zone, an integrated bracelet on a faceted case, a two-tone dress case with a crystal-set bezel. The range runs from €60 to €107, and it is the brand we stock most deeply.

What the Money Buys at This Price

The clearest way to answer that is with the watches themselves. At the bottom of the range the Navimarine NMM1011 is €60 (5,500 Lek): a slim minimalist steel dress watch with a pale ice-blue dial, applied baton indices, a polished steel bracelet and a quartz movement, with no date window to clutter it. One step up, the NM181-03 at €70 (6,500 Lek) buys the sport shape: a rotating blue bezel, a navy dial, luminous round markers, a yellow seconds hand and a date window, on a steel case and bracelet with a quartz movement. The NM001404 in blue at €75 (6,900 Lek) buys the integrated-bracelet look that normally starts much further up a price list, with a faceted case, a deep blue sunburst dial, a date window and a quartz movement. At the top, the 001808 in gold-tone at €107 (9,900 Lek) has a black 24-hour numbered bezel, luminous round markers, a date window, a fourth arrow-tipped hand that reads a second time zone against that bezel, a five-link jubilee bracelet and a quartz movement.

Put plainly, the money buys metal and design. It does not buy a movement anybody collects, and it does not buy a name that means anything to a watch enthusiast. Whether that is a good trade depends entirely on which of those you were shopping for.

What We Will Not Claim About a Navimarine

A review that only says good things is an advertisement. These are the things we deliberately do not print about this brand, and the reason for each.

The crystal. Navimarine does not publish a crystal type and nobody here has verified one at the bench, so you will not read the word sapphire on this page attached to a Navimarine. We say it about Hislon because every Hislon prints SAPPHIRE on its dial. We do not say it here, because we would be guessing, and a guess about scratch resistance is the kind of thing you find out was wrong only after the first scratch. Ask us about a specific model and we will tell you exactly what we know.

The water rating. A stated 5 ATM rating appears on the Steel 5ATM Blue, the NT0021-1, the NAVI S046 and the NMM1031, and that is fine for swimming at the surface. It is not stated on the rest of the range, and we will not spread one model's number across a whole brand to make the shelf sound waterproof. None of them is a dive watch.

The badge. Not Swiss, no documented heritage, no movement anyone would call collectable. If one of those three is what you are actually buying, Navimarine is the wrong shelf, and we would rather tell you that before you pay than after.

What We See When One Comes Back

Selling a watch and servicing it are two different jobs, and doing both is what makes this section possible. A shop that only sells sees a watch once. We see them again.

The routine job on a Navimarine is a battery, every couple of years, which takes minutes at the counter and comes with a look at the gasket while the back is open. The steel cases and bracelets take normal wear the way steel does: they pick up marks, they polish back, the clasps keep closing. The honest exception is the gold-tone models, where the finish is a plated surface layer and not solid metal. Navimarine says so in its own description of the gold NAVI 009, and so do we: keep the plating away from salt water and it will hold its finish.

The other honest thing to say is what happens if a movement ever fails outside the guarantee. A quartz movement at this price is a part, not a project. Nobody rebuilds one, here or anywhere. It gets replaced, and we quote you that before we touch the watch rather than selling you an overhaul that costs more than the watch did. Every Navimarine we sell is brand new with a 1-year Iglisi Watch guarantee and one free battery change.

Where Navimarine Is Genuinely Good

Steel, and enough of it

Solid stainless steel cases and bracelets, at a price where plated cases and folded bracelets are common. On the steel models there is no plating to wear through, which is the main reason they age better than the price suggests.

Shapes that usually cost more

An integrated bracelet on a faceted case, an octagonal bezel, a rotating dive-style bezel, a 24-hour bezel with a second time-zone hand. These are design languages you normally start paying for several tiers higher up.

Cheap to keep

Quartz means you wear it and forget it. No winding, no regulation, no expensive service schedule. A battery every couple of years is the whole maintenance story, we do it in minutes, and the first one is free during the guarantee.

Where It Is the Wrong Watch

If you are buying a name

Navimarine is not Swiss and we cannot document its history. If the label is part of what you want, the Swiss-marked watches here are Hislon and Cortébert, and you should be looking at those instead.

If the crystal decides it

There is no published crystal type for this brand and we have not verified one. If scratch resistance is your first requirement, buy a Hislon, where SAPPHIRE is printed on the dial and we can say the word without qualifying it.

If it lives in the sea

Only the models named above state a 5 ATM rating, and none of the range is a dive watch. The gold-tone pieces have plating that salt water will eventually reach. A watch that swims every day is a different purchase.

Frequently Asked Questions

No. Navimarine is not Swiss, and a nautical-sounding name is not a country of origin. The Swiss-marked watches on our shelves are Hislon and Cortébert.
