A Watch for a Milestone Birthday: 18, 30, 40, 50 and 60

A round number asks for a different watch than an ordinary year does. Here is what fits each decade, what it costs on our counter in Durrës, and where the extra money actually goes.

7 min read August 2026

An ordinary birthday and a round-number one are not the same job. Nobody keeps the gift from their thirty-seventh. They do keep the one from their fiftieth, and they usually remember who gave it. That changes what is worth spending and it changes what the watch has to survive, because it is going to be looked at again in ten years. Five decades, five answers, all from what is actually on our counter in Durrës.

Why a Round Number Changes the Gift

Two things change. The watch gets looked at more than once, so finish matters in a way it does not on an everyday gift, and the crystal in particular decides whether it still looks new later. And the gift gets attributed: people remember who gave them the watch they wore through their forties. That is worth a tier more than you would otherwise spend, and it is the whole reason the sections below climb rather than staying flat.

What does not change is our advice on the rest of it. If they already wear one watch every day and love it, a milestone is not a reason to override that, and our general birthday guide makes the same case at more length.

Eighteen

Usually the first proper watch somebody owns, so it needs to survive being owned by an eighteen-year-old. Steel bracelet rather than leather, because a bracelet rinses clean and we can take links out of it for free.

The Philippe Lauren Steel Classic at 58 euro (5,400 L) is the plainest thing here and the hardest to get wrong: stainless steel, round case, silver sunburst dial, applied baton indices.

The Casio A159WA at 61 euro (5,600 L) is the retro steel digital that has barely changed since the early 1980s. Steel case and bracelet, black bezel, alarm, stopwatch, day and date. It is the one an eighteen-year-old is most likely to actually want.

The Navimarine NT0021-1 at 75 euro (6,900 L) steps up to a blue sunburst dial, a day window at twelve and a date at six, on a steel bracelet, rated 5 ATM.

Thirty

Thirty is the first watch somebody buys with their own money, or should be. As a gift it wants to be a step above what they would pick for themselves, which in practice means a chronograph or a multifunction dial rather than three plain hands.

The Philippe Lauren Black Chronograph at 72 euro (6,600 L) is black ion-plated steel with a black dial, three chronograph sub-dials and luminous applied indices.

The Daniel Klein Exclusive Chronograph at 89 euro (8,200 L) has a deep blue sunburst dial, three subdials and a rotating dive-style bezel.

The Navimarine NAVI 009-COL 6 at 95 euro (8,800 L) is the large gold-tone multifunction: black bezel, black dial, subdials for day, date and 24-hour time.

Forty

Forty is where a complication starts to be the point. Something on the dial that does more than tell the time is what separates a fortieth-birthday watch from a good everyday one, and we have exactly two at this level.

The Cortébert Oval Moonphase at 141 euro (13,000 L) is the one people stop at: polished oval steel case, champagne dial, Roman numerals, a day subdial, a pointer date and a moon phase window at six, on a black leather strap.

The Pulsar Crystal Chronograph at 141 euro (13,000 L) goes the other way, steel with a crystal-set bezel, a white dial with three counters and a date, rated 100M on the dial and the caseback.

Fifty

The birthday people keep the gift from. This is where the Hislon range belongs: Swiss brand, sapphire crystal, a fluted bezel and a date, in three finishes so you can match what they already wear rather than guess.

The Hislon Classic Queen at 169 euro (15,600 L) is the ladies' piece in full gold-tone steel, mother-of-pearl dial with crystal-set indices, sapphire crystal, blued hands.

The Hislon Classic in gold-tone at 189 euro (17,400 L) is the same Classic in full gold-tone: fluted bezel, horizontal-stripe silver dial, sapphire crystal.

The Hislon Classic in two-tone at 199 euro (18,400 L) splits the difference, a silver case with gold-tone accents, hands and indices.

Sixty

At sixty the useful question is not what impresses, it is what gets read at a glance without hunting for reading glasses. A clear dial, real contrast between the hands and the background, and a date big enough to be worth having.

The Casio MTP-B145D at 89 euro (8,200 L) does that cheaply: a striking green sunburst dial, faceted bezel, luminous baton indices, date at three and 50m water resistance, on an integrated steel bracelet.

The Hislon Classic in steel at 199 euro (18,400 L) does it properly. Understated stainless steel, fluted bezel, horizontal-stripe silver dial, sapphire crystal and a date. It is the most legible watch we sell that also reads as a serious gift.

What the Extra Money Actually Buys

Above about 13,000 Lek on our counter you are buying two things, and it is worth knowing which.

Sapphire crystal, which is the part that decides whether the watch still looks new in ten years, because the crystal is what gets scratched. On our counter that means the Hislon range and the Romanson BH3054GBR at 74 euro (6,800 L), which is Swiss quartz with sapphire in a gold-plated rectangular case and is the cheapest way into it by a distance. We do not claim sapphire anywhere else, because it is not there.

And finishing: applied indices rather than printed, a dial with real texture, a bracelet that tapers. The Hislon Women Classic Queen at 149 euro (13,700 L) is the clearest example of the jump, in steel rather than gold-tone. Our guide to what each price tier buys takes that apart properly.

Presenting It

Three things worth knowing, none of which costs extra.

  • Some models come in their own box and some do not. We will tell you which before you order rather than after.
  • We can leave the price off everything that goes with the watch.
  • A card with your own message goes in with it if you want one.

Frequently Asked Questions

What watch do you give for an 18th birthday?

Something they can wear every day for the next ten years rather than something ceremonial. A plain steel dress watch or a steel-braceleted digital both work, and both sit under 7,000 Lek on our counter. An 18th is usually the first proper watch somebody owns, so durability and a bracelet we can size for free matter more than the brand name on the dial.

What is a good watch for a 50th birthday?

This is where gold-tone and sapphire crystal start to earn their place. The Hislon range is what we reach for: Swiss brand, sapphire crystal, a fluted bezel and a date, in steel, gold-tone or two-tone depending on what they already wear. Expect 15,600 to 18,400 Lek. A 50th is the birthday people keep the gift from, so it is worth spending past the everyday tier.

How much should an 18th birthday gift cost?

Less than people think. Something around 5,400 to 6,900 Lek buys a watch that looks like a considered present and does not look like a compromise, and at that age a watch that survives being knocked about is worth more than one that cost double. Spend the difference on a bracelet rather than a strap, because a bracelet is what we can adjust free at the counter.

Is a Swiss watch worth it for a milestone birthday?

For a round number, yes, and for one reason that is not the flag on the dial: sapphire crystal. It is the part that decides whether a watch still looks new in ten years, because it is the part that gets scratched. On our counter sapphire means the Hislon range and the Romanson, and we do not claim it anywhere else because it is not there.

Can you have it engraved?

Not in our workshop, no. We do not offer engraving and we would rather say so than send you away disappointed. There is a jeweller in Durrës who does it well and we are happy to point you at them once you have the watch. What we can do is leave the price off and put a card in with it, which is usually what people actually wanted.
